The Camel's Shop

The camel who signed for you is open for business. It has been open for forty-one days.

Night.

Nobody blinked. Nobody could see anybody. Harold, who had been up for forty-one days, finally went home.

The desert got cold, the way deserts do at night. It never snowed. The penguin sat under the Great Oak in its scarf and mittens and shivered, delighted. It hadn’t needed snow. It had needed night.

The camel unfolds the delivery slip. “Five billion acorns, one penguin (fragile),” it reads. “Harold only stays up while somebody’s watching him. I didn’t want to spoil it.” The squirrels have arrived for their acorns. They are moving into the oak.
