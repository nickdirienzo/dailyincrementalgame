Fitch’s Board

Chalked on a crate lid. Fitch takes lemons. Later he takes other things.

Sunup.

The order shipped at three in the morning. Fitch kept his word: he rode the dumbwaiter up with you, past the crates, into the grove. It was dark. There was frost on the grass and fog in the rows, and no sun anywhere.

So you sat on a crate and waited, because what else was there. At 6:47 the fog went pale and the sun came up over the lemon trees, and your eyes, trained on a billion lemons’ worth of light, could look right at it.

“Coulda come up any morning, kid,” says Fitch. “It does this every day. Six forty-seven, this time of year.” You ask why he never said. “You never asked. You had a promise. Folks with a promise don’t ask.” It was the wrong goal all along. You didn’t need a billion lemons. You needed to stay up past your shift.

It looks like a lemon, you think. A big one. Nobody’s sorted it.
